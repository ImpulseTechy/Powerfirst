Step-down (buck)

LM2596 module

₹44 ✓

Claimed3 A
Realistic2 A in still air
Vin 4.5–40 V · Vout 1.23–37 V adj · 150 kHz, non-sync
η ~80% at 12→5 V, ~73% at 12→3.3 V
Use it whenThis is the default cheap buck for a 1–2 A rail where efficiency is not critical. Enormously common, well understood, hard to kill.
Watch out forThe “3 A” on the listing assumes a heatsink. Above 2 A it gets genuinely hot. The 150 kHz switching frequency means a physically large inductor and more output ripple than modern parts.

Step-up (boost)

MT3608 module

₹35

Claimed2 A
Realistic0.8–1 A output at 5 V from 3.7 V
Vin 2–24 V · Vout up to 28 V
Use it whenSmall step-up jobs — a sensor, a small display, an LED string.
Watch out forThe 2 A on the listing is the internal switch current, not the output current. This is the single most misrepresented module on the market. A boost also draws more current in than it delivers out, so 5 V at 1 A means roughly 1.6 A from a 3.7 V cell.

Buck-boost

TPS63020 board

₹250

Claimed2 A
Realistic1.5 A
Vin 1.8–5.5 V · Vout 1.2–5.5 V · η ~92%
Use it whenYour input crosses your output. A 1S Li-ion runs 4.2 V down to 3.0 V, so a fixed 3.3 V rail from one cell needs buck at the start of the discharge and boost at the end. Neither a buck nor a boost alone can do it.
Watch out forCheck first whether you need it at all. If your load accepts a voltage range covering the cell’s range, connect the cell directly and skip the converter entirely.

Linear

AMS1117 module

₹15 ✓

Claimed1 A
Realistic425 mA dropping 5 V → 3.3 V at 35 °C
Dropout 1.1–1.3 V at 800 mA · Iq 5 mA · SOT-223 90 °C/W
Use it whenYou already have a clean 5 V rail and need a few hundred mA of 3.3 V beside it. Cheap, tiny, everywhere.
Watch out forTwo things. The 1 A rating is what the silicon passes, not what the package can cool — the heat sum gives about 425 mA. And with 1.1–1.3 V of dropout it cannot make 3.3 V from a Li-ion cell, which sits at 3.7 V. It works on the bench from 5 V and fails on the battery.

Use it whenTeaching, bench work, or a low-current rail from a modest input. Bulletproof and forgiving, with built-in thermal shutdown.

Charging

TP4056 module (with protection)

₹38 ✓

Claimed1 A charge
Realistic1 A (Rprog 1.2 kΩ ≈ 1 A)
Chemistry 1S Li-ion · Terminates 4.2 V · Rprog sets rate
Use it whenCharging any single Li-ion or LiPo cell. Buy the version carrying a DW01 and FS8205A — that adds over-discharge, over-current and short protection. The bare version has none of it.
Watch out forIt charges only. Your circuit cannot run properly off the cell while it is charging — that needs a power-path charger. And it terminates at 4.2 V, so it will overcharge a LiFePO4 cell, which must stop at 3.65 V.

Mains AC-DC

Safety — read before you build anything on mains

Never build a non-isolated mains circuit on perfboard. 230 V will kill you, and perfboard has neither the creepage distance nor the mechanical protection to be safe with it. Use a certified adapter or a potted isolated module. Both are cheap and both are already tested.
